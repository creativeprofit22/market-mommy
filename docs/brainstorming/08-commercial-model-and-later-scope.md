# Commercial model and later capabilities

## Separate business stages from payment plans

Business stage determines guidance, language, and relevant actions. A payment plan would determine access or usage. Do not assume a beginner must buy an inferior version of the advice or that an experienced user needs every advanced feature.

Pricing and packaging are open hypotheses, not settled decisions.

## Updated commercial focus

The earlier discussion proposed an agency/B2B opportunity-intelligence wedge. The subsequent direction prioritizes people finding a business idea or seeking first customers. Preserve the earlier idea as a possible later market, not the current default audience.

For beginners, test whether a bounded one-off guided outcome is more understandable and valuable than an ongoing subscription—for example, evaluating a direction and preparing a first offer. Do not promise that purchasing guidance produces a customer.

Recurring value may become clearer when someone needs ongoing opportunity discovery, sales improvement, monitoring, and outcome learning.

## Commercial questions to validate

- Will the intended user pay for this specific help?
- Does it improve decisions enough to justify both price and time spent?
- Can the user carry out the advice with their resources?
- What is the full cost of collection, models, support, and review?
- Which benefits recur often enough to justify a subscription?
- Does a human-assisted service help establish quality before deeper automation?

Bound usage and paid research; unlimited scraping and agent runs can hide unsustainable costs. Avoid optimizing engagement at the expense of user progress.

## Possible longer-term packaging

A workspace subscription with bounded monitoring/research allowances is one hypothesis. Analyst-assisted services and narrowly defined guided packages are other hypotheses to test in sequence, not a finalized menu.

The strongest potential differentiator is a permissioned record of evidence, decisions, actions, and outcomes—not scraped data volume or a graph alone.

## Scope buckets

| Focus first | Add as users progress | Preserve for later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finding a realistic direction | Comparing customer segments | Detailed company/person relationship views |
| Selecting a problem | Improving pricing | More complex forecasting |
| Creating a clear offer | Repeat business and referrals | Multi-team planning |
| Finding appropriate buyers | Competitor monitoring | Large-scale market monitoring |
| Testing willingness to pay | Income/cost and capacity scenarios | Advanced attribution and simulations |
| Recording real outcomes | Consistent sales processes | Enterprise reporting and controls |

## Preserved advanced vision

- Account and opportunity monitoring across multiple sources.
- Public professional relationship graphs with provenance and time-aware edges.
- Forecasting specific commercial events and testing against historical baselines.
- SaaS product, retention, integration, and pricing decisions.
- Agency offer development, buyer triggers, and repeatable delivery economics.
- Content strategy informed by evidence and connected to commercial outcomes.
- Partner and distribution analysis.
- Approved integrations with execution destinations such as Linkgo.
- Hosted monitoring for unattended jobs.
- Broader agent interoperability where useful.

These are not a backlog commitment or approved Roadmap. A later feature should be introduced only when it solves a demonstrated user problem and earns its cost.

## Shared foundation remains

Moving downmarket changes the questions, research depth, presentation, and commercial packaging. It does not discard ggframework, programmatic controls, CLI access, evidence retrieval, or provider evaluation. See [technical architecture](05-technical-architecture.md).
