# Beginner product contract

Status: audience and delivery accepted on 2026-09-18; bounded journey specified for future implementation, not built. Approval provenance and remaining decisions: [decision ledger](decisions.md). Numerical workload and acceptance targets belong only to [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## Audience and income needs

Start with English-speaking adults in the United States who have an existing deliverable skill, limited money they can afford to lose, and a 30–60-day planning horizon. Start with a small skill-based service where delivery and buyer access can be checked, not an obligation to build software. This horizon is a planning constraint, not a forecast of first earnings. Record the person's actual deadline, desired take-home amount, available hours, spending tolerance, reachable buyers, location, and relevant qualifications; allow unknown answers.

Someone needing immediate money for essentials is a mismatch for speculative business testing. Explain the uncertainty, do not recommend debt or paid research as a rescue, and allow exit with their information preserved. Suggest considering dependable paid work or verified local assistance without inventing eligibility or availability. Regulated, unsafe, or qualification-dependent work cannot proceed on an assumption of permission; stop that direction pending qualified review. US scope does not establish compliance with state or local rules.

## Complete bounded journey

| Step | User action and useful output | Failure or correction path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand situation | Answer progressive questions, including “I'm not sure”; save a correctable profile and constraints | Missing critical constraints prompt a focused question; urgent-income mismatch leads to safe exit |
| Check evidence | Review permitted first-party material and bounded research scope, sources, expected sharing and cost before any external call | No provider or source approval means manual/fixture path only; failed checks are not evidence of no demand |
| Find a direction | Review a small shortlist: buyer, problem, deliverable, fit, costs, delivery effort, buyer access, evidence and counterevidence | Insufficient/conflicting evidence yields a gap or low-risk information-gathering action, not an invented opportunity |
| Choose and correct | Select a direction or correct assumptions; retain prior recommendation versions | Reinterpret suitable existing evidence first; flag stale dependencies and collect again only if justified |
| Prepare an offer | State who buys, what they receive, exclusions, estimated delivery effort, starting price and expense assumptions | Unknown costs or qualifications remain visible; no false margin or fixed-price certainty |
| Prepare first-customer test | Choose appropriate reachable buyers and a human-reviewed conversation/offer; record original success and stop conditions | No automatic outreach, publishing or purchase; reject unsuitable access methods; pause if permission is missing |
| Act outside the app | User conducts the approved experiment and delivers only work they can safely fulfill | Rejection, no response or failed delivery are valid outcomes; the app cannot guarantee a customer |
| Record results | Record conversations, objections, offers, paid commitments, actual payments, refunds, expenses and hours separately | Correct duplicates or mistakes through traceable revisions; do not overwrite original experiment conditions |
| Decide next step | Continue, revise, stop, or repeat a bounded test using actual learning | No sale remains an honest outcome, not a forced success; uncertain results do not justify scaling |
| Return later | Resume saved progress, see the next action, reason and outcome entry | Interruption must not erase saved inputs or silently repeat external calls |

The digital journey ends with a saved test and later outcome/review; time waiting for buyers is not software latency. A first customer is a desired real-world outcome, not an app completion requirement. The accepted workload bounds are in [evaluation](evaluation.md#bounded-workload).

## Scope and delivery

One person's business profile and active experiment in a single-user local pilot, reached through a guided browser interface and approachable launcher. Shared TypeScript/Node application core, SQLite persistence and required ggframework adapter; CLI and GG Coder are secondary routes to the same use cases. See [architecture](architecture.md) for rationale and non-implemented boundaries.

The normal experience presents next action, why it fits, and what happened. Evidence, calculations and source limitations are progressively available. Chat can help but is not the sole navigation mechanism. See [UX/copy](ux-copy.md).

## Stage-3 extension and non-goals

Finding a direction and getting a first customer are the initial business stages. Making sales consistent is a later extension using observed outcomes to consider clearer offers, pricing, referrals, repeat work and unproductive channels. A single sale never automatically advances a stage. Preserve enough outcome history for that extension without implementing it now.

Deferred: enterprise dashboards, multi-team coordination, relationship graphs, forecasts, broad OSINT, hosted monitoring, autonomous execution, generic MCP, extra databases, Rust components and a separate desktop shell. Agencies and SaaS remain adaptable future business contexts, not the initial market definition. Content and Linkgo execution are optional later hypotheses, not a mandatory growth strategy.

## Commercial boundary

Business stages describe needs, not product payment plans. Pricing, one-off guided purchases and subscriptions remain unvalidated. Customer offer prices use explicit currency and assumptions; product operating budgets are a different quantity. No income guarantees, synthetic demand proof, fabricated testimonials or pressure to spend. A purchase of guidance cannot promise a paying customer.
